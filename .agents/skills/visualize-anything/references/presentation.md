# Presenting a visual analysis

Use the opening viewport for a question, its clearest answer, and one strong view. Make the rest of the page follow the reader's likely questions. Keep small summaries for values that matter to the story. Give plot marks space and use quieter treatments for sources and metadata.

- Choose a visual style appropriate to the material. For an evidence brief, start with a readable editorial hierarchy, a neutral surface, one accent, and direct annotations. Give categories matching colors across views. For signed changes, show direction via position and signed text as well as color.
- Let readers follow a claim to its source or sample in one action when practical. Include the population and period beside the chart. Keep important values readable without hover.
- Add interactions when they reveal new evidence, such as drilling into errors, changing a comparison, or exposing exact values. Use visible focus and meaningful control labels.
- Open the rendered artifact at a desktop and a narrow viewport when browser tools are available. Look for clipped labels, text overflow, weak contrast, missed focus, and interactions that change the chart without updating its interpretation or source. Fix what you observe before delivering.
